# Q3 video script (about 2.5–3 minutes)

**Open before recording, each in its own tab:** the Copilot chat (scrolled to the top), the Excel padlet file, your Q3 Word page, and the references page with the Copilot and Claude links.
**Record with:** Teams or PowerPoint (Insert → Screen Recording). Zoom the browser to 125%. Practise twice and say it in your own words. Don't read it word for word.

---

### 1. Intro (0:00–0:15) | Q3 Word page
"Hi, I'm [name]. I did Question 3, the critical evaluation of the padlet on inclusive software engineering. It has 45 posts from our Lecture 5 class."

### 2. Preparing the data (0:15–0:35) | Excel file
"I used the Excel export, not the padlet board. Each post is split into a Subject and a Body, and 'No data' just means one of them was empty. I merged the two parts and numbered every post P1 to P45. Numbering was important: it meant I could check exactly where Copilot put each post, instead of trusting a summary."

### 3. Copilot chat walkthrough (0:35–2:00) | Copilot chat, scroll as you talk

**Message 1: the big prompt**
"I used Copilot with my university account. In my first prompt I gave it all 45 posts and asked for 4 to 6 themes, with post numbers and counts. I added rules: every post in exactly one theme, no invented posts or quotes, and the counts must add up to 45."
*Scroll to the answer.* "It gave five themes, and the total was correct. But when I read each post myself, I found four in the wrong theme."

**Message 2: my first correction**
*Point at each line as you say it.* "P8 says diversity helps only 'if handled well', and P43 says non-diverse teams are 'not inherently bad'. Both are cautious posts, but Copilot had put them under benefits. P22 is about reaching bigger audiences and P31 is about pressure on companies, which are both business impacts, yet Business had only 2 posts. Copilot had made the padlet look more positive and less critical than it really is. I sent these four corrections with reasons, and it fixed them."

**Message 3: first mind map**
"Next I asked for a mind map as Mermaid code, using plain words only, because symbols like brackets break the code. It worked, but it came out with a dark background and the centre text was hard to read."

**Message 4: the full table**
"Then I asked for a table of all 45 posts with a theme and a short reason for each, so I could check every single post against the Excel file, not just the four I'd spotted." *Point at the P8, P43, P22 and P31 rows.* "These four are now in the right place."

**Message 5: light mind map**
"I asked for the mind map again, with a setting for a light theme so it would print clearly." *Show it.* "The counts are 12, 11, 11, 4 and 7."

**Message 6: final cross-check**
"Finally, I sent Copilot my whole manually checked grouping and asked it to compare that with its table and list any differences. It found none: all 45 posts appear exactly once and the counts match. So the final themes come from Copilot's grouping plus my four corrections, which both of us then checked."

### 4. Findings (2:00–2:30) | Q3 Word page, point at the boxes
"The mind map is the centre of my answer. The two biggest themes are diverse perspectives, with 12 posts, and accessibility and reducing bias, with 11 each. What stood out is what's missing. Only 7 of 45 posts are cautious, and none of the 45 cite any evidence. Disabled people, women and low-income users are each mentioned only once. Older, neurodivergent and LGBTQ+ users are never mentioned, even though the padlet is meant to be about impacts on people. So I compared the posts with outside evidence: the Gender Shades study, which found error rates of 34.7% versus 0.8%, and McKinsey's 39% figure. McKinsey only shows correlation, though, not cause."

### 5. What I learned about using AI (2:30–2:45)
"My main lesson is that Copilot was good at sorting and counting, but it smoothed over disagreement. It put cautious posts in with the benefits. If I hadn't numbered and checked every post, the analysis would have looked more positive than the padlet really is. Next time I'd ask for the full table first, because that's the easiest way to check."

### 6. AI use (2:45–3:00) | References page, click each link
- "Reference 5 is my Copilot chat. Copilot grouped the posts and made the mind map. I checked all 45 posts and made the four corrections I've just shown."
- "Reference 6 is my Claude chat. I used Claude to understand the task, check Copilot's grouping, suggest the Gender Shades and McKinsey sources (which I opened and checked myself), help draft the wording, and re-draw the first mind map in light colours without changing its content. I checked every quote and number against the padlet."
- If a link won't open or share: "Sharing is blocked on my university account, so I've shown the full chat on screen instead."
